# Fast teleop: torque governor + load-aware IK. Findings (2026-11-08)

The question: with a plugged-in bench supply (no current limit), how fast can teleop safely move the arm, and does
**load-aware IK** (letting the fingers tilt to take load off the servos) help?

Tables and charts: [FAST_TELEOP_STUDY.md](FAST_TELEOP_STUDY.md), generated by `sim/scripts/fast_teleop_study.py`
(~90 s). Raw numbers: `sim/out/fast_teleop/report.json`.

## Short answer

- **Speed:** the new **torque governor** makes click-to-move **18 % faster** than the fixed gentle profile
  (6.97 s vs 8.5 s over 9 moves), with lower peak torque (≤ 70 % of stall vs 74 %) and the same tracking. Keyboard
  driving at the new 0.25 m/s tracks to 16 mm on average, against 37 mm (worst 112 mm) for the gentle profile.
- **Safety:** no overload trips anywhere, even when the governor's model is wrong (arm 40 % heavier plus a
  150 g payload it doesn't know about) or the servo's stiffness and damping are off by a factor of 4 either way.
- **Load-aware IK:** a 15° tilt cone cuts the shoulder's holding torque by **8 % on average, up to 45 %**, but
  almost only **below 10 cm**, which is exactly where teleop keeps the fingers straight down for grasping. It
  doesn't lower the worst case (far reach, high up), and it made moves slightly slower. Your choice of a **0°
  default is right**; the cone stays available (`[` / `]`) for long holds at mid height.
- **Found on the way:**
  - **A real hazard in the old click-to-move.** Jumping the joint goal straight to a far target lets each joint
    move on its own, so the tip doesn't go in a straight line. From a low pose it drove the gripper into the
    table: 1.7 s pressed against it at 100 % torque, stuck 111 mm short. Clicks now travel a straight line.
  - **A modeling error in the first servo load study,** now fixed. See the correction in
    [SERVO_LOAD_FINDINGS.md](SERVO_LOAD_FINDINGS.md). Its conclusions stand.

## The power budget with a bench supply

- **Voltage: 12.0 V.** That's ~8 % more torque and speed than the datasheet's 11.1 V (3.18 N·m, 5.96 rad/s),
  and still below the HX-30HM's 12.6 V maximum.
- **What limits motion now** (`so101sim/servo_model.py`, the governor's constraints):
  - **Torque at speed:** back-EMF leaves less torque the faster a joint turns.
  - **Overload protection:** above 80 % of stall for 2 s, the servo caps itself at 20 %.
  - **Heat:** the manual's continuous rating is 1/3 of stall.
- **Current:** fast teleop peaks at ~5–6 A total, against ~3–4 A gentle and ~10–12 A for uncontrolled full
  speed. Size the supply for ~20 A, which is all six servos stalled.

## How the pieces work

**Torque governor** (`so101sim/governor.py`). This replaces the fixed speed/acceleration profile for the five arm
joints. Every servo tick (2 ms) it:

1. **Predicts each joint's torque** with the arm's dynamics model (`so101sim/dynamics.py`): inertia, gravity,
   Coriolis, friction and an assumed 50 g payload.
2. **Checks it against what each servo can deliver right now:**
   - the voltage margin, 90 % of the supply, which leaves the rest for the servo's own position loop;
   - an overload budget: at most 1 s above the 80 % threshold, half of the servo's 2 s trip time;
   - a heat budget: it slows down when RMS torque nears the continuous rating.
3. **Caps the speed** so that every joint can still hold gravity, with 10 % of stall to spare.
4. **Shapes the command** with a smooth, critically damped reference (35 rad/s) and scales the whole arm's
   acceleration so every joint stays inside its limits.
5. **Slows down if the measured arm lags** the command by more than 3°. This guards against model error.

**Straight-line moves** (`so101sim/motion.py`). Click-to-move and `H` move the tip *target* along a line at up to
0.8 m/s. The line advances only as fast as the **measured** arm keeps up: if different joints lag by different
amounts, the real tip leaves the line.

**Goal interpolation** (`servo.GoalInterpolator`). IK runs at the 30 Hz frame rate and the servos at 500 Hz. Each
new IK goal is spread over one frame instead of stepping.

**Load-aware IK** (`ArmIK.solve(..., tilt_tol_deg=…)`). This is a 5-DOF arm, so placing the tip leaves one free
direction. Strict IK uses it to point the fingers down. Load-aware IK lets the fingers tilt within the cone and
uses that direction to minimise the sum of squared holding torques instead. It converges in about 15 iterations
(3–11 ms).
- In teleop the cone ramps from 0° at 5 cm tip height to full at 10 cm, so grasps are always straight down.
- 0° runs the old solver unchanged.

## Results

**Click-to-move** (9 moves across the workspace, including a wide pan; HX-30HM at 12 V):

| motion | total time | peak torque (shoulder / elbow) | overload trips | max tip lag | peak current |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| gentle profile (2.75 rad/s, 8 rad/s²) | 8.50 s | 44 % / 74 % | 0 | 7 mm | 3.7 A |
| **torque governor** | **6.97 s** | **69 % / 57 %** | **0** | **7 mm** | **5.1 A** |
| max speed (no limits) | 5.51 s | 100 % / 100 % | 0 | 39 mm | 10.1 A |

- **Max speed** is 21 % faster than the governor but runs both joints at full torque, lags up to 39 mm and draws
  10 A. It's also not repeatable: the lag depends on the load.

**Robustness** (governor):

| real arm vs the governor's model | total time | peak shoulder | trips |
|---|---|---|---|
| as modelled | 6.97 s | 69 % | 0 |
| +20 % link mass, 100 g unknown payload | 6.97 s | 88 % | 0 |
| +40 % link mass, 150 g unknown payload | 7.00 s | 100 % (for 0.07 s) | 0 |

| servo loop (unknown, swept) | gentle time / peak | governor time / peak |
|---|---|---|
| default (full voltage at 2°, D 17 ms) | 8.50 s / 74 % | 6.97 s / 69 % |
| stiff (0.5°) | 8.06 s / **100 %** | 7.00 s / 72 % |
| soft (5°) | 8.81 s / 46 % | 7.21 s / 62 % |
| no derivative term | 9.08 s / 57 % | 7.34 s / 79 % |

- **The fixed profile isn't automatically safe.** Its peak torque depends on the servo it meets: a stiff servo
  pushed it to 100 %.
- **The governor adapts to whichever servo it meets** and stays at 62–79 %.

**Keyboard streaming** (the target slides along a path):

| key speed | gentle: mean / max error | governor: mean / max error |
|---|---|---|
| 0.10 m/s (old default) | 6.6 / 18 mm | 6.6 / 10 mm |
| 0.25 m/s (new FAST default) | 37 / 112 mm | **16 / 27 mm** |

**Load-aware IK** (holding torque, 30 g payload, tip anywhere in reach, no height ramp):

| tilt cone | shoulder torque saved, mean / max | elbow torque change, mean | worst-case shoulder |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0° | — | — | 30 % of stall |
| 15° | 8 % / 45 % | +4 % | 29 % |
| 25° | 17 % / 72 % | +8 % | 26 % |

- **Where the savings are:** see `fast_load_aware_static.png`. They're largest low and mid-reach, at heights of
  5–10 cm.
- **Little effect high up:** strict IK already has to tilt there (wrist_flex at its limit).
- **In motion it was no help:** clicks took 7.51 s with the 15° cone vs 6.97 s without, and streaming errors
  were the same.

**Wrist roll twin** (the 180° roll that gives the same jaw line, camera on the other side):
- It's inside the camera-safe roll range for only 23 % of rolls, and saves at most 4 %.
- Not used: a 180° flip of the wrist view isn't worth that.

## Things that went wrong on the way (and what they taught)

1. **Bang-bang acceleration rang the servo.** The servo's position loop is lightly damped (~7 Hz), so a
   time-optimal "max acceleration, then max braking" command spiked actual torque to 100 % while the motion
   itself needed 13 %. The fix is a smooth (critically damped) command; 50 rad/s already rings the wrist servo.
2. **Stop-and-go at the frame rate.** A goal that steps every frame makes a fast generator catch it, stop and
   restart 30 times a second. Fixed by interpolating goals.
3. **Feeding the goal's velocity forward** made it worse (that velocity steps every frame). It was removed.
4. **Pacing the path on the command, not the measured arm,** let the real tip leave the line and hit the table.
   The path is now paced on the measured arm.
5. **Joint damping was double-counted** in the servo model (above).

## Recommendations

1. ✅ **Teleop defaults to FAST** (governor, 0.25 m/s keys, 0.8 m/s straight-line clicks). `G` switches to
   GENTLE.
2. ✅ **Tilt cone defaults to 0°** (your choice; the data agrees). Use `[` / `]` for long holds at mid height.
3. **On the real arm,** start with the governor at its defaults, but:
   - first measure each servo's step response, which gives `sat_error_deg`, `d_time_s` and the loop's ringing
     frequency;
   - raise `bandwidth_rad_s` only if the loop is faster than the sim's;
   - feed the governor the servos' **measured positions** (lag guard and path pacing need them);
   - log present current (register 69) and compare it with the governor's predicted torque, which validates the
     model.
4. **Not worth doing:** flipping wrist roll for load, or moving the ESP32 board off the wrist.

## Caveats

- **The governor is only as good as its model.** The sim tests it against the same rigid-body model (plus mass
  and servo-loop errors), not against real gearbox friction, backlash or compliance.
- **The real servos' stiffness, D gain and overload semantics are unmeasured.** The study sweeps the first two.
- **The heat budget is untested in these short tasks:** the RMS window is 2 minutes.
- **Move times include settling** to within 5 mm/s of tip speed. Droop after settling (4–5 mm at 2° stiffness)
  is the servo's P-only hold, the same for every generator.
